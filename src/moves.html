The idea

Every machine runs at 100 %; only the number of machines of each recipe changes. The machine counts that satisfy the exact rules below form an integer lattice. Its reduced basis is a set of moves: bundles such as “+2 of this, −1 of that” that keep every exact rule exact. The page only ever changes the factory by moves, so it cannot become unbalanced.

The rules

  • Phase 5: the four Phase 5 recipes are fixed at one batch, the fewest whole machines that make the parts in exact proportion (1000 : 1000 : 256 : 200) and that whole machines upstream can feed exactly: 192 AI Expansion Servers per minute. (The Phase 5 machines alone would allow 64/min; the balances upstream force three times that.) No move changes these four counts.
  • Net 0 (tinted rows): every fluid and every intermediate that is not one of the items below.
  • Piles up: anything the build gun uses (any building cost), all ammunition, filters, and fuels (solid generator fuels, packaged fuels and batteries; nuclear fuel rods must balance). Net ≥ 0 with no minimum.
  • Raw: what comes out of miners and extractors is only drawn, in whole multiples of 30/min. Node and well limits are not modeled, nor is power.

The start uses every recipe that needs no hand-gathered items, except Unpackage recipes and the Packager recipes other than Packaged Fuel, Turbofuel, Rocket Fuel and Ionized Fuel, at the fewest whole machines that satisfy the rules (an integer program solved offline, tools/moves_start.py).

Products

The list shows every product with its net per minute and its build cost: the items it takes to construct every machine now in the factory (the machines only, not belts, power or the miners and extractors). Tier is the milestone tier of the recipe that makes the item; for items first made by a MAM or hard-drive unlock, the highest tier among what it takes to unlock and make it (research cost, prerequisites, ingredients, machine). Hover a tier for the unlock it comes from.

Selecting a product opens its move sheet: each column is one unblocked move that changes the product's net or the machines that make or use it, each row a recipe that some column changes, with its machine count now. The columns come from the product's own chains (“make more of it”), the chain moves of each recipe that makes or uses it, and the lattice basis, each tried in both directions; only those that break nothing from the current factory are shown.

Reading the recipe tab

  • Step is the smallest change in a recipe's machine count that any combination of moves can make. A big step is a chain of exact ratios that only works in large batches; a different recipe usually shrinks it.
  • Chain moves are built the way you would by hand: the selected recipe plus a supplier for each net-0 input it uses (and a consumer for each net-0 output it makes), recipes already running first, until everything balances, scaled to whole machines. The lattice moves below them are the reduced basis: every exact change is a combination of them, but on its own a basis move often touches a recipe at 0 machines and is blocked.
  • Product chains through a recipe are the “make more of X” chains that use it, found in the background.
  • Shrink this searches combinations of up to three moves (each used once or twice) for the one that lowers the selected recipe the most and keeps everything valid.
  • Adding a recipe starts it at 0 machines; a recipe can be removed once it is at 0.

The lattice is exact (big-integer column reduction, then Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász reduction). Recipe data is the production sheet's. Import takes this page's exports and production-sheet files with every machine at 100 % that satisfy the rules; export gives a production-sheet file.
