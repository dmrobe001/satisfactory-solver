The idea

Every recipe runs a whole number of machines, all at one clock you choose (default: the plan's). Fluids and unusable products must come out exactly even, and the Phase 5 parts in exact proportion if that box is ticked. The machine counts that satisfy those equations form an integer lattice. Its reduced basis is a set of moves: bundles such as “+2 of this, −1 of that” that keep every exact balance exact. The page only ever changes the factory by moves, so it cannot become unbalanced.

A move can still make something go negative: a machine count, a building material that is used faster than it is made, or a raw resource that would be produced instead of drawn. Such moves are greyed out. Raw resources and power are not limited here.

Reading the numbers

  • Step is the smallest change in a recipe's machine count that any combination of moves can make. A big step is a chain of exact ratios that only works in large batches; a valve (Items tab) or a different clock usually shrinks it.
  • The Phase 5 step is the smallest batch the current clocks allow. It shows how well the clocks fit together: rounding every clock on its own (Clean clocks) or setting them all to 100 % usually makes it very large, because the exact Phase 5 ratio needs clocks that suit one another. At the optimizer's own clocks the plan is exact, but some moves are large.
  • Shrink this searches combinations of up to three moves (each used once or twice) for the one that lowers the selected recipe the most and keeps everything valid, preferring fewer machines in all. Minimums on the Items tab (Phase 5 parts start at their current rate) stop it from shrinking what you want to keep.
  • Changing a clock, a valve or the recipe list rebuilds the moves and snaps to the nearest valid factory.

The lattice is exact (big-integer column reduction, then Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász reduction). Recipe data is the production sheet's. Import takes production-sheet exports, optimizer plans and recipe-matrix exports; export gives a production-sheet file.
