Save and load

Edits save in this browser automatically. To keep a sheet or move it elsewhere, use Export sheet and Import sheet at the top of the page (a .json file), or copy the text below and paste it into another copy.

Computed buildings

Each number is how many of that building to bill per unit of the basis named in the heading; the current basis is shown beside it. Every building’s product is rounded up to whole buildings. Buildings you place by hand go in “Extra” on the Buildings tab; a set of lights, signs, trains, drones and the like is filled in there by default.

How the sheet updates

A recipe runs when its clock is above zero. Changing a recipe’s clock or adjustment re-sizes it: it gets just enough whole machines to cover what the running recipes consume of its first output, plus that output’s build need, plus its adjustment. Then each of its inputs is checked. If exactly one running recipe makes that input, that producer is re-sized the same way, and so on up the chain. If two or more running recipes make an input, the one among them with its Flexible box ticked is re-sized instead; if none or several are flexible, none of them is touched, and the shortfall shows in yellow on the consumers and red on the producers. Ticking Flexible re-sizes that recipe straight away. You can type any recipe’s machine count; its suppliers follow.

Power is treated as one more input. A single running generator recipe follows the power demand automatically. With several generator recipes running, the one flexible generator follows it; otherwise a power shortfall marks all of them red.

An item’s target production is everything the running recipes consume of it, plus its build need (from the bill of materials and the build time), plus the adjustment. The adjustment may be negative, for example to accept a shortfall you supply from elsewhere. The item’s producer gets enough machines to meet the target, rounded up to whole machines at the current clock. Build need depends on the buildings, which depend on the machine counts, so the sheet repeats this until the two agree. Surplus machines is how many machines a recipe runs beyond the fractional number its first output’s target needs, with the other producers as they are; a negative value means the item is short. If several running recipes share an output, the largest of their adjustments applies. A by-product also drives sizing when its recipe is the item’s designated producer.

Modelling choices

  • Machine counts are whole numbers, rounded up, so most recipes run a small surplus. Lower the clock to trim it. Generators take no adjustment; they follow power demand.
  • Consumer power scales as clock1.32; generator output and fuel use scale linearly. Converter, Particle Accelerator and Quantum Encoder use the average of their power cycle.
  • Alien Power Augmentors add 500 MW each and multiply total grid production by 1 + 10% (or 30% when fed 5 Alien Power Matrix/min) per augmentor. Augmentors, the Main Portal (250 MW, 2 Singularity Cells/min) and the Resource Well Pressurizer (150 MW) take a typed machine count.
  • Resource well extractors, geothermal generators and hand-gathered items have a fixed clock: on or off.
  • Hand-gathered items (leaves, wood, mycelia, remains, power slugs, Somersloops, Mercer Spheres) count as layer 0 with one “gatherer” per item per minute and no building.
  • Foundations, walls and roofs cost their base Concrete (Silica for windows) plus 2 of the material’s item: Iron Plate for FICSIT, Steel Beam for Steel, Grip Metal and Metal roofs, Plastic for Coated Concrete, Concrete for Concrete, Asphalt and Tar, Silica for Glass roofs.
  • FICSMAS recipes and buildings are hidden and count for nothing unless the FICSMAS box is ticked; that includes the ordinary materials they would consume. FICSMAS Gifts are hand-gathered, one gatherer per gift per minute.
  • Layer 0 is every recipe without inputs. A recipe is in layer N when all its inputs can come from layer N−1 or below.
